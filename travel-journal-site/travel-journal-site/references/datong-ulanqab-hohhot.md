# Datong — Ulanqab — Hohhot

Use this reference for the complete route or any of its three chapters. Read only the sections relevant to the requested work.

## Route narrative

Build one continuous transition:

1. Datong: stone, ancient architecture, earth, and historical weight.
2. Ulanqab: wind, open land, geological terrain, and an expanded horizon.
3. Hohhot: northern city life meeting grassland cultural memory.

Connect the chapters with one route line and a palette transition from warm stone, through mineral grey and open-sky blue, toward deep blue, grass tones, warm white, and restrained gold.

## Datong

- Character: historical weight, stone construction, caves, walls, timber structures, dry northern light.
- Palette: sandstone, loess beige, charcoal, dark timber, muted vermilion.
- Motifs: stone layers, architectural grids, cave silhouettes, carved shadows.
- Photography: frontal architecture, material detail, strong light and shadow.
- Motion: slow reveals, layered shadows, restrained vertical movement.
- Map: warm neutral terrain with dark routes; fade architectural grids or stone texture into the boundary.
- Avoid imitating an antique interface.

## Ulanqab

- Character: wind, open land, distant horizons, grassland, and volcanic terrain when present in the actual journey.
- Palette: volcanic black, mineral grey, dry grass, cold sky blue.
- Motifs: horizon lines, wind traces, geological contours, sparse road markers.
- Photography: wide compositions, large sky areas, distant subjects.
- Motion: gentle horizontal drift and sparse wind-like movement.
- Map: increase negative space; use subtle contours and dark terrain without reducing route readability.
- Avoid literal animated grass or exaggerated wind effects.

## Hohhot

- Character: contemporary city life, northern landscape, and grassland cultural memory.
- Palette: deep sky blue, grass green, warm white, dark indigo, restrained gold.
- Motifs: broad curves, roofline rhythm, sparingly used textile geometry.
- Photography: city–landscape contrast, evening light, human-scale details.
- Motion: calmer transitions, wider arcs, and a sense of arrival.
- Map: gradually increase labels and urban detail while retaining colors from the preceding landscape.
- Avoid reducing local culture to decorative symbols or tourist clichés.

## Transitions

### Datong to Ulanqab

- Fade stone texture into geological contours.
- Shift warm beige toward mineral grey and sky blue.
- Widen the composition and increase negative space.

### Ulanqab to Hohhot

- Move from volcanic grey toward grass tones and deep blue.
- Reduce wind movement as the route approaches the city.
- Increase urban detail gradually rather than switching themes abruptly.

## Mobile

- Present each city as a clear chapter.
- Keep route progress visible but compact.
- Replace continuous map/content overlap with controlled stacked states when space is limited.
- Disable pointer-only atmosphere on touch devices.

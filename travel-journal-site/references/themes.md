# Destination Themes

Use this reference when adding a destination or changing a page's atmosphere. Load only the relevant destination section. These directions are starting points rather than rigid presets.

## Yichun — forest

### Character

Quiet forest roads, cool mist, dense tree cover, and a slow, restorative rhythm.

### Visual language

- Palette: pine green, moss, fog grey, warm wood, and restrained cream.
- Motifs: forest canopy, contour lines, mist, bark texture, and winding roads.
- Photography: deep natural layers, filtered light, road-scale details, and generous negative space.
- Typography mood: calm, spacious, and literary.

### Motion and map

- Use slow parallax, soft fades, and restrained pointer disturbance.
- Keep motion organic rather than springy or mechanical.
- Use subdued roads over deep green terrain.
- Let fog-like gradients soften the boundary between the map and article content.

## Dalian — coast

### Character

Open coastline, sea wind, changing light, harbor infrastructure, and a brighter travel rhythm.

### Visual language

- Palette: sea blue, sand, white, slate, and restrained sunset coral.
- Motifs: coastline curves, waves, harbor lights, sea haze, and coastal roads.
- Photography: open horizons, reflective water, geometric harbor details, and evening light.
- Typography mood: clean, airy, and slightly more energetic than the forest theme.

### Motion and map

- Use gentle horizontal drift and tide-like transitions.
- Avoid literal or cartoon-style wave animation.
- Use pale coastal terrain with an emphasized shoreline.
- Let translucent sea-color overlays connect the map to adjacent content.

## Adding a destination

Create a short section containing:

1. The emotional character of the journey.
2. A restrained palette derived from the real landscape.
3. Two to five visual motifs.
4. A photography and typography direction.
5. Motion and map treatments connected to the place.

Keep the site's shared navigation, spacing logic, and interaction language intact. A destination should feel like a new chapter of the same book, not a separate product.
